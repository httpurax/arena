# Armor and Outfits

Outfits are your life support in the Zone: they blunt bullets, shrug off
anomaly burns, and carry the artifact containers that make artifact builds
work. Like weapons, they degrade — and a suit at low condition protects
far less than its stats suggest.

## Outfit condition

- Every suit has a condition bar. Protection values scale with condition,
  so a half-ruined suit guards noticeably worse than a fresh one.
- Repair before condition drops to around half; beyond that, technician
  prices climb steeply.

## Protection types

- **Ballistic protection** reduces damage from bullets and shrapnel.
- **Anomaly protection** covers burn, chemical, electrical, and psi
  damage from anomaly fields and emissions.
- **Radiation protection** slows the rads you accumulate from hot zones
  and from the artifacts you carry.
- No single suit is best at everything — match the suit to the job:
  combat suits for firefights, scientific suits for anomaly fields.

## Repair

- Technicians repair suits for currency, with cost scaling by missing
  condition and suit value.
- **Sewing kits** and **duct tape** patch up suits in the field for small
  condition restores — good maintenance between town visits.
- **Armor repair kits** (basic / advanced / expert tiers) are used at a
  workbench and carry limited charges; partial use across several items
  is more economical than maxing one suit.

## Upgrades

- Technicians upgrade suits along a tree, gated by the same three
  toolkit tiers as weapons: tools for rough work, tools for fine work,
  and the calibration toolkit.
- Common upgrade categories include extra artifact containers, improved
  anomaly or ballistic protection, reduced weight, and better stamina
  recovery while wearing the suit.
- Additional artifact containers are among the most valuable upgrades —
  each extra slot multiplies what an artifact build can do.

## Artifact slots

- Artifacts can only be worn safely in a suit's artifact containers; the
  number of slots depends on the suit and its upgrades.
- More containers means stronger combined artifact effects — plan suit
  upgrades around the artifact set you want to run.

## Helmets

- Suits with integrated head protection cover the head slot on their own.
- Suits without it can be paired with standalone helmets, which add their
  own anomaly and psi protection on top of the suit's stats.

## Exoskeletons

- Exoskeletons are the heaviest class: top-tier ballistic protection and
  huge carry capacity, at the cost of speed, stamina drain, and noise.
- Servomotor and frame upgrades improve mobility and load bearing, making
  an exo far more practical for long hauls.
- An exo is a late-game investment — buy it once you can afford the
  repairs and the upgrades that tame its drawbacks.

## Disguise system

- Wearing another faction's suit marks you as a member of that faction.
  If your own faction sees through it — or if you never removed the
  original faction patch — expect friendly fire.
- Strip or swap the faction patch when wearing a captured suit if you
  want to pass unnoticed.

## Tips

- Keep two suits if you can: a combat suit and an anomaly suit, and swap
  per task.
- Upgrades raise repair costs, so fully kitting a suit you will replace
  soon is wasted money.
- Duct tape is light — always carry some for field patching.
