# Crafting and Repairs

G.A.M.M.A. (via Anomaly) has a full do-it-yourself economy: workbenches,
repair kits, disassembly, and crafting recipes let a prepared stalker
maintain top-tier gear for a fraction of technician prices — if you put
in the legwork.

## Workbench and vice

- Crafting, disassembly, and repair-kit use happen at a **workbench**;
  many recipes also require a **vice**.
- Workbenches are found at faction bases and some neutral settlements.
  Using a vice may cost a small fee.
- Keep an eye out for workbenches while traveling — the closest one
  decides whether DIY maintenance is practical on a given run.

## Repair kits

- Kits come in **basic**, **advanced**, and **expert** tiers, roughly
  matching the tier of the gear they service.
- Each kit has a limited number of charges. You do not need to spend a
  whole kit on one item — spread charges across several worn items, then
  finish each with maintenance items.
- As a rule of thumb: use kits to bring an item back to a maintainable
  level, then keep it there with oil, tape, or sewing kits.

## Maintenance items

- **Gun oil / cleaning kits**: restore small amounts of weapon
  condition. Best used little and often on lightly worn guns; they
  cannot rescue a ruined weapon.
- **Sewing kits** and **duct tape**: field repairs for outfits, good
  for small condition top-ups between town visits.
- **Multitool**: a general crafting and repair aid worth keeping in the
  pack for workbench sessions.
- These items are common loot — pick them up instead of buying them.

## Disassembly

- Unwanted weapons and outfits can be broken down at a workbench into
  components: barrels, receivers, springs, fabrics, and similar parts,
  roughly grouped by weapon family or caliber.
- Yields are random per item — disassembling one gun does not guarantee
  any specific part. Dismantle in bulk for reliable stockpiles.
- Strip attachments before disassembling: scopes and silencers are
  worth more intact or reused than as scrap.

## Crafting

- Recipes consume components to produce repair kits, upgrade parts,
  consumables, and more.
- High-tier crafted upgrades are expensive in parts — a top-tier weapon
  upgrade can require dozens of a single salvaged part type, so hoard
  early.
- Ammo crafting exists but is resource-hungry with poor exchange rates;
  it is mainly worthwhile for rare ammo types you cannot buy reliably.

## Technician vs DIY

- Technicians charge currency scaling with missing condition and item
  value — fast and simple, but costly for high-tier gear.
- DIY with kits and parts is far cheaper for expensive weapons and
  suits once you have the components stockpiled.
- Delivering the three toolkit tiers (rough work, fine work,
  calibration) to a technician unlocks upgrade tiers and a permanent
  discount — do this at your home base early.

## Tips

- Loot everything maintainable: oil, tape, sewing kits, and multitools
  are free if you pick them up.
- Never pay a technician for what a few charges of a repair kit and
  some oil can fix.
- Stockpile disassembly parts long before you need a tier-3 upgrade —
  farming them last-minute means grinding kills for drops.
